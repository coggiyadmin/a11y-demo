Does the sign-language interpretation convey the same information as the audio?

Method: guided-manual · Criteria: 1.2.6 · Automated outcome: cannot-tell

Why automation cannot decide this

A tool can detect that a sign-language track is present. It cannot judge whether the signing is complete, correctly timed, in the sign language the audience actually uses, or large and well-lit enough to read. Sign languages are distinct languages, not transliterations of the spoken track.

When this does not apply

This criterion is inapplicable inapplicable when there is no prerecorded audio in synchronised media. Recording inapplicable is a correct result here, not a gap.

How to check it

  1. Identify which sign language the audience uses; it is not implied by the page language.
  2. Watch the interpretation against the audio and check nothing is summarised away.
  3. Check the interpreter stays visible, well lit and large enough to read at the default size.
  4. Check the interpretation is synchronised with the audio it interprets.

Evidence to capture

The media, the sign-language track, and a note from a fluent signer.
